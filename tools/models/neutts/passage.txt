The old lighthouse stood at the edge of the cliff, its white paint peeling after decades of salt and wind. Every evening, Margaret climbed the narrow staircase to light the lamp, counting the steps as her father had taught her. There were one hundred and twelve of them. From the top, she could see the fishing boats returning to the harbour, their small lights bobbing on the dark water. Some nights the fog rolled in so thick that the beam seemed to vanish a few metres from the glass. On those nights she stayed awake, listening for the horns of ships that might have lost their way. In the morning, she would write everything down in the logbook: the weather, the boats she had seen, and anything unusual. Nobody had read those pages in years, but she kept writing anyway.
